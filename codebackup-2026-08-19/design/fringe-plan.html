The Open Fringe

One continuous city map outside the yard — real streets, real buildings, armed patrols. No boxed areas, no walls except the ones that make sense. The tutorial was small on purpose; nothing after it needs to be.

SCRAPPED: the four-boxed-areas plan and the Approach as built — it was junkyard tiles, junkyard props and junkyard robots rearranged into a square. Deleted in step 1. The area system underneath it survives, but only for interiors and, later, other rings.

Orientation first. The city on the atlas is six rings; everything on this page is the south-east slice of the outermost one — the ground you cross before the Sprawl even begins.

That last row is the strategy in one line: we build corridors through a city that only looks complete. The rings get smaller as you go inward, so each one costs less than the last — and the Core is a single room.

The Fringe itself · street by street

The model · one open map, not rooms

The whole Fringe wedge becomes a single continuous world you can walk across. Interiors (shops, houses, the church) are the only "areas" — you step through a door and the inside loads, exactly like the shack does today.

The gas station · first real POI

The landmark you see from the gate road, and the plan's proof that a POI can carry a whole encounter without a quest attached to it.

  1. The canopy. A wide roof on pillars — the first structure you walk under. Rain-shadow, dark, echoing.
  2. The pumps. Four islands. They explode — the same barrel system, scaled up: a pump chain-detonates its neighbours. The best and most dangerous cover in the ring.
  3. The shop. Enterable interior: shelves stripped, one aisle untouched, a till drawer forced. Loot, and a sign that says what it sold.
  4. The residents. HHD patrol uses the forecourt as a waypoint. Fighting here means fighting around live fuel.

New enemies · HHD — Human-Hunter-Droids

Scrappers are yard machines: junk, no weapons, no purpose beyond being in the way. The street belongs to something built for the job. HHDs are the first machines the AI made to hunt people — and they carry guns.

UnitRoleBehaviourFeel
HHD-1 "Marshal"Armed gruntPatrols the street on a route, rifle at rest. On detection: shoulders the weapon, fires 3-round bursts with a visible laser line, backs off to keep range, takes cover behind trafficThe first enemy that shoots back properly. Two of them is a real fight
HHD-2 "Bailiff"Close pressureShotgun-ish spread at short range; charges when you hide, forcing you out of cover the Marshal is watchingThe pair is the point: one pins, one flushes
SpotterFlying eyeUnarmed drone that circles high, sees far, and calls HHDs to your position — killing it fast is the whole skillTurns stealth from optional into urgent
Scrapperyard onlyStays behind the junkyard fence where it belongsYou've graduated

They also change how the map plays: HHDs hold positions (junctions, the forecourt, the church approach) rather than wandering, so the street network becomes a set of contested crossings — and the alleys and shop interiors become the way around them.

Engineering for open world

Honest list. None of it is exotic; all of it is required before a 200×150 map is playable at 60fps.

WorkWhat it isWhy now
Viewport tile cullingCompute the visible tile range from the camera instead of looping the whole gridToday we touch every tile every frame — fine at 1,024, wasteful at 30,000
Spatial prop bucketsProps bucketed into 16×16-tile cells; only nearby cells get drawn and depth-sortedSorting thousands of props per frame is the one thing that would actually break performance
Windowed minimapPre-render the whole map small once, show a scrolling window around the playerA 200×150 minimap can't sit in the corner at 1.5px/tile
Procedural block fillHand-place the street network and POIs; generate terraces, alleys, and clutter from a block library seeded per districtThe only way one person authors 30,000 tiles at quality
Region save statePosition-keyed records (already how barrels and items work) extended to cleared POIs and looted interiorsSave v4, migrating v3 runs intact — the rule stands
Distance ambienceWind, distant machinery and reverb shifting by district; night streetlight poolsA big map is dead without local atmosphere

Build order · each step playable

  1. Delete the Approach. Strip the rejected area; keep the area system for interiors.
  2. Open-world engine. Tile culling, prop buckets, windowed minimap — proven on an empty 200×150 test map that runs at full speed.
  3. Street tileset + block library. Road, kerbs, pavement, verges; facades, streetlights, traffic, signage. The look lands here.
  4. The gate road + gas station. First playable slice of the real Fringe: walk out, follow the road, find the station, read the signs.
  5. HHDs + Spotters. The street gets dangerous; the junction-holding behaviour ships with them.
  6. The rest of the network. Terraces, parade of shops, depot yard, and the roads reaching Candlelight, Field 12 and the Underpass.
Calls I need from you: map size — is 200×150 the right ambition, or should the first open build be tighter (say 140×110) and grow? · do HHDs drop their guns as the ring's weapon upgrade (I think yes — a rifle from the first thing that shoots at you is a perfect reward) · should night fall as you travel, so streetlights and headlights matter, or stay permanent dusk?
