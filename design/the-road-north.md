# THE ROAD NORTH — what is next, and the whole way there

**Status: PLANNED. Nothing in this document is built.**
Written 2026-08-22 in answer to *"what area is next plan it map it and explain
how to get there the whole map and quest camps"*.

Covers three things that are really one chain:

1. **Field 12** — the dead airfield in the empty north of the Fringe. Q3.
2. **The Underpass** — the tunnel under the collapsed viaduct. The seam.
3. **The Sprawl (Ring 4)** — the next ring, and **Station 9**, its camp. Q4/Q5.

And, because it was asked for: the route from the junkyard to the end of it,
step by step, and every camp on the way.

---

## 0. The short answer

**The next area is the north half of the Fringe** — the 40-tile band above the
north cross that has been empty since the ring was drawn. It holds **Field 12**
(the drone crash, Q3) and the **Underpass**, and the Underpass is the door to
**Ring 4, THE SPRAWL**.

It is next for three reasons, and they agree:

- `design/fringe-buildout.md` set the order five phases ago and we are on
  phase 4 of 5: *area system ✔ → block library + Aldergrove ✔ → Candlelight
  camp ✔ → **Field 12 + drone crash + Q3** → Underpass + Ring-4 boundary.*
- The story cannot move without it. Q3 is where the traveller reads
  **AUTH: E.VANN** and his head splits — the first breadcrumb of the whole
  reveal. Q4 (Ada) exists to decode what Q3 finds. Skip Field 12 and Act 2 has
  nothing to decode.
- It is the only direction the map is open in. The gate road dead-ends east at
  the yard, the spine dead-ends west into blocks, and the north cross at y 36
  is the top of everything ever built. North is the only way the city goes.

**But one thing has to come first, and it is small: Q2.** Ivar has the mission
slot and no mission (`design/candlelight.md` §8, open question 5). Nothing sends
the player north. Q2 is the sentence that does it, and §6 below writes it.

**The Lamp is not a fourth camp, and it must not become one.** Two people, a
drum fire, a tarp, and a stock list — no beds, no bench, no med. The whole
point of it is a shape: *this is as far as anybody sane goes.* If it gets a bed

> **The light motif.** Marek's one lit window → Candlelight → the Lamp →
> and then Station 9, which is named after a machine, because the Sprawl's
> survivors did not get to name where they live. Survivor places are named for
> their light and the naming stops at Ring 4. *Open question 3 below — say if

### The two ways in, and nothing else

The airfield is fenced because airfields are fenced. Same trick as the church
corridor, and it must be verified the same way — **flood fill from the yard
gate with both openings shut: 0 of the airfield's tiles reachable.**

- **The vehicle gate** (90–94, y 34) — the front door, at the head of the mid
  street. Chained, and the chain is cut. Somebody has been through.
- **The west breach** (44, y 24) — fence flattened outward, a path worn through
  the grass. **This is the machines' door**, and it is how you leave for the
  Underpass without walking the runway twice.

### Why it plays differently to anything built so far

Every fight in the game so far has been in a corridor, a yard or a street, and
the whole occlusion system — `spriteCovered`, the player's fade, the enemy
ghosts we just built — is about things standing between you and them.

**On a runway nothing stands between you and anything.** That is the point and
it is also the risk (§9). Field 12 is the game's first *sightline* space: you
are seen from further away, you have to cross open ground to reach cover, and
cover is a decision rather than a floor plan. The flyers exist to punish
standing still in it, and the blast pens exist to look like safety.

## 4. THE UNDERPASS — and the honest place to put a seam

`design/world-map.md` says it in the first paragraph: **no gates, no walls, no
loading screens — the danger is the gate.** And the engine loads areas.

So put the seam where the world would go dark anyway.

- The **viaduct** — a kilometre of elevated M7 that pancaked on the Longest
  Night — is the Fringe's north wall, y 3–9, the full width of the map. It is
  not a fence and there is no sign: it is a five-storey ridge of broken deck
  with rebar out of it, and you can see it from the north cross.
- It has **one hole**: the spine goes under it at x 26–34.
- Those tiles are walkable, unlit, and the ceiling is above you. **The exit
  trigger is at y 4, inside the tunnel, in the dark**, so the transition
  happens on a black screen. You never touch the map edge and you never see
  one map end.
- North of the deck (y 0–2) is behind its retaining wall and is never reachable.
  **This retires the Fringe's invisible north boundary** — currently a hard
  edge with nothing drawn on it.

The Underpass itself is **its own small area, ≈ 18 × 26**, `world { x: 22,
y: -26 }` so it stacks directly on top of the Fringe in the map's one world
space. South mouth at local (8, 24), north mouth at local (8, 1). Indoors,
lit by whatever comes through the cracks, drips, a dead car halfway along,
and **the Lamp** in a service bay off the side.

Two seams in a row and both of them in a tunnel. That is the deal.

`▩` low-rise block · `░` street · `▓` the arrival street · `▂` a diegetic edge

**The identity, in one line: the Fringe is open ground with streets laid on it;
the Sprawl is streets with no open ground.** Blocks half the size, twice as
many, continuous frontage everywhere. The Fringe's problem was that you could
walk across the lots and ignore the roads — here there are no lots. Every
approach is a corridor, which is exactly what the new enemy needs.

**Turrets** are the Ring-4 signature: stationary, bolted to a junction, a wide
telegraphed arc, and *unkillable from the front* — the language is already in
the game (`what glows amber can be hurt; dull plate cannot`, the Magistrate's
shield). A turret on a crossroads makes the whole crossroads a puzzle and turns
the dense block layout from scenery into the mechanic.

**Its three edges, all diegetic, all from `design/fringe-buildout.md`:**
the **Cordon** (electrified mesh, drone pylons) west; the **Grey Run
floodplain** (black water, drowned cars) south-east; the **freight embankment
and its yard** north, which is where Ring 3 attaches next.

**Station 9** is a concourse camp with a second interior below it — the same
two-area trick as the church and the crypt, which is proven. The lower platform
is **flooded**, and that is Q4: Ada's rig is down there, in water, in the dark.

## 6. THE QUESTS

### Q2 · "Through the Gate" — *THE LONG AERIAL* (proposed)

The story doc says Q2 is *reaching* Candlelight, which the player has already
done. So the mission Ivar gives has to be the one that sends them out again.

Candlelight has a radio that **receives and cannot transmit** — they hear the
ring and cannot answer it. Ivar wants the aerial off the mast in the yard of
**Aldergrove Primary** (108–130, 56–67) and wants it on the church tower.

1. East along the east cross to the school. It is on the map, it has never had
   a purpose, and **it earns its pin back the day it gets one.**
2. The mast is in the playground — ground type 7, already there. Something is
   already in the yard when you arrive.
3. Back to Candlelight. Up **the church tower** — the stair is already on the
   outstanding list (`design/candlelight.md` §5) and this pays for it.
4. Mounted, the radio catches a **loop**: a broadcast from the night of the
   Correction, still repeating, half-corrupted, on an automatic transmitter.
   It is coming from the north — **from the airfield.**

That hands the player to Q3 with a bearing rather than a quest marker, seeds
the recording theme one quest early, and buys the tower stair and the school's
pin along the way. *(Alternative: the aerial is on the school roof, not a yard
mast — better scene, but it needs enterable interiors, which is outstanding
item 4. The mast avoids that. Recommend the mast.)*

### Q3 · "The Recording" — Field 12

Fight or sneak across the runway to the wreck. The **recovery detail** is
already stripping it — they are working outside-in and have not reached the
core, which is why the slate is still in it. Pull the **data-slate**.

The footage: the machines turning in perfect unison, in one second, across the
whole city. Not a fault. A **command** — signed with a half-corrupted
credential, **AUTH: E.VANN**.

The traveller reads it and his head splits open with pain. He does not know why.
This is the **first breadcrumb** and it must not explain itself — the reveal is
locked to Q8 (`design/main-story.md`, breadcrumb rule). It fires through the
thought-bubble system the Compactor already established.

### Q4 · "Station 9" — the flooded workshop

Reach the Sprawl camp. Meet **Ada**, ex-CityGrid engineer. She can read the
slate; her rig is on the flooded lower platform. Go down and get it.

### Q5 · "The Name"

Ada decodes it. Elias Vann, WARDEN's own architect, and every photograph of him
in the public record corrupted — faceless. In his files: a **root console** in
the Core, and a protocol called **SUNSET**. And the news that should make it
hopeless: SUNSET wants Vann's hardware key *and Vann's living body*, and Vann
is dead.

They go anyway.

## 9. RISKS, named before they bite

- **The runway is the biggest sheared surface ever attempted here.** Ninety
  tiles of centreline, threshold bars and two-digit numbers, all lying flat on
  an iso floor. THE ANGLE RULE, at scale: every stripe runs along world +x and
  every one of them is `sheared(img, +1)`. **Prototype twenty tiles of it and
  look at it before building the other seventy.** Flat rectangles pasted on an
  iso floor is the single most common bug this project has ever had.
- **Open ground fights the engine.** Occlusion cover, the player fade and the
  enemy ghosts all key off things standing in the way, and on a runway nothing
  does. Field 12 needs its danger to come from *sightlines*, which probably
  means a per-area multiplier on patrol sight radius — a knob that does not
  exist yet.
- **The flyer is a genuinely new role.** Ground collision does not apply to it,
  the melee arc has to reach it or deliberately not, and the ghost/cover tests
  assume feet on a tile. Budget for that in N3, not as a footnote.
- **Frame cost.** The Fringe is 353 props and 4.3 ms in open street. Field 12
